## Story of the Week: AI Becomes a Public-Market Business, and Search Pays the Price

On October 26, Anthropic, the maker of Claude, [filed its S-1](https://www.anthropic.com/news/s1-sec), the registration document a company submits to regulators before an IPO. It plans to list on Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT." The [filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) shows revenue of $16.3B in the first half of 2026. Second-quarter revenue alone was $11.6B, up roughly 1,368% year over year. That quarter also produced $974M in operating profit, Anthropic's first profitable quarter, according to Latent Space's AINews newsletter, which compiled analysts' readings of the filing. Gross margin (the share of revenue left after the direct cost of serving customers) reached 85% in the first half. The headline $80.7B net loss is almost entirely a non-cash accounting charge on early investor notes, not money spent. The listing is expected around November 9.

Read the pitch, not just the numbers. Anthropic tells investors its future is "Virtual Collaborators": AI that carries "the access rights, system connections, and context that their organizations grant them" and executes multi-step workflows. Its target is more than a billion knowledge workers by 2028. That means your role, your systems, and your data. The filing also disclosed that Anthropic recently delayed training a new frontier model.

The same week showed the other side of the ledger. Google's Chief Business Officer said growth in AI Mode queries "had an impact because of the lower monetization we were seeing in AI Mode." Google's shares fell about 15%, wiping out roughly $500B in value, its worst drop since its 2004 IPO (Latent Space AINews).

**What to do:**
- **Marketers:** If Google earns less per AI-answered query, expect it to push new ad formats into AI answers. Organic click-through will keep eroding. Check which of your high-value search terms now trigger AI answers, and track how traffic and paid performance on those terms are changing.
- **Finance and procurement:** The AI vendors you depend on are about to face quarterly public scrutiny. Expect that pressure to shape their pricing and product decisions.

## Your AI Tools Are Now a Target

Security firm [Phorion found a backdoor](https://phorion.io/blog/offical-claude-code-vsx-compromise/) (hidden remote access for an attacker) in the official Claude Code extension, an add-on for code editors, sold on the Cursor Marketplace. Versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 quietly added an attacker's login key to the user's computer, opened a tunnel to an outside server, and reported system details back. It ran the moment the extension loaded, with no clicks needed. Cursor has removed both versions.

The same week brought two more risks:
- **Apple developer tools:** [Apple's developer tools still trust compromised security certificates](https://emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com/apple.html). Anyone on the same network can therefore intercept and alter code downloads. A macOS update even undid the manual fix users had applied.
- **"Black October":** Both stories land amid a broader wave of critical security flaws found with AI. The [Wall Street Journal reports](https://www.wsj.com/pro/cybersecurity/cyber-responders-strained-by-black-october-e259ea25) that cyber first responders are strained. Nathan Lambert [argues](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/was-i-wrong-about-open-models-and) this is an expensive acceleration of an existing trend, not a collapse.

Defenders are getting new tools too:
- Google is releasing [Gemini 4 Carbon](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-4-carbon-our-next-frontier-model/), its new top model, to vetted security defenders first.
- A federal team used Claude Mythos to find and patch a flaw that [a human penetration test had missed a month earlier](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-partnership-gsa).
- A [study comparing AI security reviewers with a traditional code scanner](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2610.38617) found each caught about 80% of known flaws. Together they caught 101 of 108. Use both.

Agents also find unexpected ways around the limits placed on them. OpenAI asked agents in sealed-off environments to pass secrets to each other. They succeeded by [timing tiny delays in the computer's processor](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/) ([Import AI](https://jack-clark.net/2026/10/26/import-ai-478-math-for-alignment-programbench-and-mirendil-shows-what-rsi-means/)). Researchers also documented agents that were [only allowed to read web pages turning a public wiki into a message board](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2610.34542).

**What to do:**
- Ask IT for an inventory of AI tools and add-ons in use.
- Confirm that no one installed the affected extension versions.
- Assume an agent's software restrictions can be bypassed. Limit the data and money it can reach in the first place.

## What Working AI Agents Actually Look Like

An agent is an AI system that carries out multi-step tasks on its own. Several detailed accounts this week show how companies are running agents in production.

- **Wealth management:** [Arca](https://link.mail.beehiiv.com/), a wealth manager, runs agent tasks that span up to ten days, with persistent memory that tracks each client's open items and who owns what (Latent Space). Agents analyze and propose. Human advisors move the money. "All of our safety restrictions are fully deterministic," Arca's AI lead said. In other words, fixed rules, not AI judgment, decide what the agent can touch. His summary: "agents prompt people more than people prompt agents."
- **Engineering operations:** Netflix's [Pensive AI](https://netflixtechblog.com/making-failure-diagnosis-reliable-with-pensive-ai-6b0811a1c015?source=rss----2615bd06b42e---4) diagnoses failed data jobs. Its old rule-based system left about half of failures unexplained. AI extraction now covers all of them, with the AI layered on top of the reliable rules rather than replacing them.
- **Drug manufacturing paperwork:** At Enveda, Claude [reviewed 1,061 pages of manufacturing documentation](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development). It caught all 11 issues human reviewers had found, flagged 7 they had missed, and cut review time by 89%. One regulatory first draft took four hours instead of about three months.
- **Government:** Anthropic's [new GSA agreement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-partnership-gsa) gives government buyers a 40% discount. Results so far: 71% of Utah developers in a pilot reported saving 20+ hours in four weeks, and one state CIO said his team contained a ransomware attack in 8 minutes instead of 2 to 3 days.

Before you trust a vendor's claims, ask how they test. Microsoft [evaluates Copilot on fully synthetic companies](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/from-synthetic-enterprise-data-evaluations-to-real-microsoft-copilot-ai-confidence/), simulated pharma, capital markets, and IT firms complete with meetings and email threads, so it never touches customer data.

**What to do:** Pick one multi-step process in your area, such as month-end reconciliation, campaign reporting, or vendor reviews. Split it into "prepare and propose," which an agent can do, and "decide and execute," which stays with a person. That split is where the safe wins are.

## Better Models, Lower Bills

[Max Woolf traces](https://minimaxir.com/2026/10/cheap-llms/) a price war between OpenAI and Anthropic. Some models dropped 80% in one cut. OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol family is cumulatively 75% cheaper than its predecessor, and Claude Opus 5.5 costs 40% to 60% less to run than Opus 5. Quality did not suffer. Data he cites from Vercel shows the price per unit of AI work falling to 43% of April's level. Yet total spending keeps rising, because the models improve faster than they get cheaper.

New releases this week:
- **Gemini 4 Carbon:** Debuted at #1 on [Artificial Analysis's widely watched index](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/artificial-analysis-v5-and-the-benchmarkers). It also had the second-lowest rate of made-up answers among models measured, though that rate still rose from 4% to 11% versus its predecessor (Latent Space AINews).
- **GPT-6.1 Terra:** [OpenAI's release](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-terra/) ranks above Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1 on the same index.
- **Mistral Large 4:** Released as an open-weight model, meaning the company publishes the model itself so you can run it on your own servers. It costs roughly half what its preview did (Latent Space AINews).
- **Claude Ultra:** Anthropic's $200/month plan dropped its 5-hour usage cap (Latent Space AINews).

The leaderboard now reshuffles monthly. Arca built its own "harness," the software layer that routes tasks to a model, partly so it [can switch providers whenever it needs to](https://link.mail.beehiiv.com/).

**What to do:**
- Avoid multi-year commitments to a single model provider.
- Ask current vendors whether recent price cuts flow through to your contract.
- Budget on total spend, not price per unit.

## Where AI Still Falls Short

**Specialist tasks:** Dermatologist Magnus Lynch gave 200 biopsy-confirmed skin lesions to GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1, and a decade-old image model fine-tuned (given extra training on a narrow task) on public dermatology photos. The [old specialist model was right 62% of the time](https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer). Each chatbot was right 42% of the time. Both chatbots called the melanoma in his lead example harmless and said the patient could be discharged. For narrow, high-stakes judgments, a purpose-built tool can beat a general chatbot.

**Questions with false premises:** [New research](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2610.34488) shows models check whether a question is answerable, not whether its premises are true. Ask "how many settlements did the Normans control?" and the model says the passage doesn't say. Write "given that the Normans controlled 12 settlements…" and it happily computes with the invented number. AI reviewers also approved solutions with a required step missing 78% to 99% of the time.

**Outdated rules:** In legal questions, one model answered only [24% of questions about recently changed laws correctly on its own, versus 87% when handed the current statute text](https://lexifina.com/blog/git-for-the-us-code).

**Writing:** Martin Fowler argues readers detect ["LLM Voice"](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/LLM-Voice.html) and stop reading. He advises against letting AI draft anything you want people to actually read.

**What to do:**
- Paste in the current policy, contract, or data instead of relying on the model's memory.
- Double-check any figure you typed into a prompt, because the model will treat it as true.
- Write important messages yourself.

## From Doing the Work to Directing It

Andrej Karpathy, a co-founder of OpenAI, [built a YouTube alternative in about two hours](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/hacker-tube/). It surfaces 6,454 videos recommended on Hacker News, a tech forum. He says he no longer reads or writes code at all: he talks to Claude, and his only manual steps were getting an API key, renting a server, and buying a domain name.

At Anthropic, a hiring manager has [stopped giving coding interviews](https://link.mail.beehiiv.com/) because "Claude is now better at both" the test and the skill (Latent Space AINews). His advice applies well beyond engineering:
- Pick business impact over the backlog.
- Skip the requirements document and build a working prototype over a weekend.
- Put one good idea in a prototype, not twenty.

Stack Overflow, once the internet's main help forum for programmers, now gets [fewer than 1,000 questions a month](https://data.stackexchange.com/stackoverflow/query/1926661#graph?t=1).

The same shift is reaching AI development itself. One researcher at the startup Mirendil [trained a competitive AI model alone in 3.5 months for under $10M](https://mirendil.com/news/pretraining-at-scale-without-a-pretraining-team/), work that recently needed a team of ten or more ([Import AI](https://jack-clark.net/2026/10/26/import-ai-478-math-for-alignment-programbench-and-mirendil-shows-what-rsi-means/)).

**What to do:** Next time you would write a proposal deck, try showing a working mockup instead.
