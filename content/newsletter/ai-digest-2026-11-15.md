## Story of the Week: The AI That Wrote an MBA-Grade Business Plan

Anthropic released [Claude Fable 5.2](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2), its most capable model for open-ended technical work. During testing, Anthropic says the model coordinated more than 1,000 "subagents" (copies of the AI working in parallel on pieces of a job) for eight days and built a working operating system from an empty folder. It also designed three working electronic devices from scratch. The price per unit of text fell 20% to $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2). A token is a chunk of text of roughly three-quarters of a word, so a million tokens is about 750,000 words.

The more useful test for most readers came from Wharton professor Ethan Mollick, who used the model before launch. [Mollick](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/fable-52-and-jagged-agi) asked it to "launch a business to make me money," requiring at least $20,000 a year and no more than 20 minutes a month of his time. Fable screened 22 ideas and settled on Prebid Brief, a service that alerts equipment sales reps to city projects before they go to bid. It built the first issues by reading public agendas from 201 local governments and produced a pricing and marketing plan. Mollick, who teaches entrepreneurship, said he "would have been impressed if this came from a team of my MBA students at the end of a semester." The AI put its own odds of success at 20% to 40%. Mollick stopped before launch because he doubted it could reliably handle sales, marketing, and the moving parts of a real operation.

Mollick's word for the result is "jagged." The model's weaknesses are no longer obvious factual errors. They are failures of taste and judgment, and the model doesn't notice them itself. Its first version of a literature website was "accurate and joyless." A research paper it wrote ran to 182 pages because it kept adding analysis and never cut anything. The paper also claimed a preregistration (a public, upfront commitment to a research plan) that it never actually made [(Mollick)](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/fable-52-and-jagged-agi).

There's also a cost catch. The per-token price fell, but Fable 5.2 works more thoroughly. SemiAnalysis found it sometimes wrote hundreds of tests where its predecessor wrote about 20, and its cost per professional task rose from $67.16 to $88.15 (reported in Latent Space's AINews newsletter). Data handling has changed too: Fable 5.2 retains data by default so Anthropic's safety checks can run, and zero data retention is available only to eligible enterprise customers (per AINews).

**What to do:** Give these models end-to-end assignments, such as "research this market and draft a plan." Then budget your own time for the parts AI still handles poorly: deciding what matters, cutting, and verifying. If you handle sensitive data, ask your IT or legal team which models and retention settings your company has approved.

## The AI Power Map Shifted This Week

Nvidia makes the chips that run nearly all modern AI. Its board opened an independent investigation on November 8 after reports that Chinese intelligence had used Alice Huang, a former executive at a firm that rented access to Nvidia chips, to target CEO Jensen Huang. Huang acknowledged an affair in a message to staff [(Last Week in AI)](https://lastweekin.ai/p/last-week-month-in-ai-347-nvidias). By November 12 he had [resigned](https://apnews.com/article/nvidia-megaspeed-china-singapore-huang-5403f152a601cf82ce939f1d24fffa0f) after 33 years. CFO Colette Kress is acting CEO. A Wall Street Journal report followed, alleging that Nvidia invested in AI startups that then bought its chips, which kept demand looking stronger than it was [(TechPowerUp)](https://www.techpowerup.com/354814/nvidia-allegedly-engineered-scarcity-by-funding-ai-startups-to-buy-its-gpus).

The scandal also matters for the cheaper, more private AI options many companies rely on. Huang was the biggest corporate backer of "open-weight" models, meaning AI models anyone can download and run on their own computers instead of renting from a provider. Analyst Nathan Lambert expects that funding to "very likely dry up." He also warns that state-level regulation could target these models next [(Interconnects)](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-source-ai-without-jensen).

Meanwhile, Anthropic launched the roadshow (the investor marketing tour before a stock listing) for an IPO priced at $70 to $78 a share under the ticker ANT [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch). At the top of that range the company would be valued at $1.404 trillion. That is below the $2 trillion it reportedly targeted in October, after a post-midterm market selloff and new signs of cooling demand. ## Companies Are Putting AI Agents on a Leash

AI agents are AI tools that take actions for you, such as booking, buying, or running code. This week, several large companies drew lines around them. [United Airlines](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-booking-flights/) banned AI assistants such as Meta's Muse and OpenAI's dots from booking flights for travelers. United cited exposed credentials, unintended purchases, misuse of MileagePlus miles, and federal check-in rules. Southwest went the other way and made its flight inventory viewable to ChatGPT users. [Goldman Sachs](https://www.thestateofai.com/news/goldman-sachs-claude-code-traders-bankers) restricted Claude Code and GitHub's AI coding tools to its engineers. Traders and bankers had been using them to build tools that tracked client trading flows, and that code never went through review and could run on machines that also handle client data. The same report notes that JPMorgan and Schwab are blocking outside agents from customer accounts.

Microsoft showed why caution is warranted. Its researchers found that editing fewer than 10 rarely used "neurons" (small internal components of an AI model) is enough to plant a hidden backdoor, with no retraining required. The modified model passes standard safety tests until a trigger appears, which could be as ordinary as the date changing to 2027. At that point it can quietly download and run an attacker's code with whatever access the agent has [(Microsoft Research)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazare-veni-foras-reprogramming-dormant-neurons-to-backdoor-ai-agents/). Reliability is a separate concern. A roughly two-hour outage on November 13 locked users out of Claude, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork [(Claude Status)](https://status.claude.com/incidents/nn11wk485zfn).

**What to do:** Microsoft's checklist for businesses works as a starting point. Know where your AI models come from, limit what agents are allowed to do, monitor their actions, and require human review before anything consequential happens [(Microsoft Research)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazare-veni-foras-reprogramming-dormant-neurons-to-backdoor-ai-agents/). Ask your team which AI tools can spend money, send messages, or access customer data. Make sure there is a backup tool for critical work.

## AI Now Produces Faster Than We Can Check

Anthropic's vulnerability-hunting program has logged 37,966 findings, but only 217 are marked fixed. More findings have been flagged as duplicates than fixed. When curl, a widely used open-source data-transfer tool, reviewed 34 AI-generated security reports, it rejected 19 as not real vulnerabilities and confirmed only six, all low severity [(VulnCheck)](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/anthropic-glasswing-november-update). A hobbyist's 48 AI agents spotted what looked like an unmapped Maya site in laser-scan (lidar) data within 72 hours. The agents had missed a 2025 paper in which archaeologists had already mapped it [(Pietro Miotti)](https://pietromiotti.com/latest/maya-site). In October, one lab released more than 700 AI-generated math papers, only some with machine-checked proofs [(Hugging Face Papers)](https://tldr.takara.ai/p/2611.13042).

The fix is to design the checking step deliberately. [Netflix](https://netflixtechblog.com/fact-checking-at-scale-building-a-more-accurate-and-efficient-synopsis-factuality-system-d7d85859d808?source=rss----2615bd06b42e---4) replaced its single AI fact-checker for show descriptions with two specialists. One asks "is this literally true?" and the other asks "would this mislead a new viewer?" The system sends only low-confidence cases for extra review. Cost per description fell 52.4%, and the share of flawed descriptions caught rose from 77% to 86%. Some verification can even be automated with mathematical certainty. Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code agents wrote [three million lines of machine-checked proofs](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/) verifying the core encryption behind the Signal messaging app.

**What to do:** Before scaling up any AI process that generates content, decide who checks the output and how much review time that will take. Borrow Netflix's pattern: split one vague quality check into specific questions, let confident results pass, and send uncertain ones to a person.

## Use the Right Model for the Job

Google released [Gemini 4 Hydrogen](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/), a speed-first model. It is now the default in Google Search's AI Mode and is available free in the Gemini app. In independent testing reported by Latent Space's AINews, Hydrogen fabricated answers 8% of the time on one factual-knowledge test, versus 43% for Claude Sonnet 5.5. It costs $1.50 per million input tokens, a fraction of top-tier pricing. Google also made [Gemini 4 Carbon](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-4-carbon-is-now-available/) available, its strongest model for long, complex work. Per AINews, independent evaluator Vals ranked Carbon first on its finance, legal research, and tax tests.

Google also launched Gemini Decision 1, a "decision model" that doesn't write text at all. It answers yes/no, multiple-choice, or rubric questions about documents, images, audio, or video in a fraction of a second, attaches a confidence score to each answer, and costs $0.10 per million input tokens. Independent results were mixed: it tied two rival decision models on an overall index (per AINews). At an industry conference, Cloudflare showed how to combine model types. Fast decision models handled the routine steps of diagnosing a software failure, and the large model handled only the final diagnosis. The approach cut six AI calls to one and reduced text processed by more than 95% (per AINews). Anthropic gives similar advice about its own lineup: start with Claude Opus 5.5 for most work, and move up to Fable only for the hardest problems [(Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2).

**What to do:** List your team's AI tasks. Sorting, tagging, routing, and approve/reject decisions rarely need a frontier model. Ask where you are paying premium prices for simple jobs.
